# Cheaper Booking Playbook

How the same experience is bought for less. Use this to fill `local_alternative`
on every paid option.

## The ladder, cheapest first

1. **Free access** — many "tours" only add commentary to a place that is free
   to enter. Say so, and move the activity to `free_visits` instead.
2. **Public transport + free site** — a bus and an entry ticket often replace a
   60 € "excursion" that is mostly transport.
3. **Official site of the monument or museum** — no commission, and the
   skip-the-line ticket is usually the same one the platforms resell.
4. **Local agency, harbour kiosk, hotel desk** — boat trips, diving, jeep
   safaris and day trips are sold on the spot, typically 20-40 % below the
   platform price, often the same operator and the same bus.
5. **International platform** (GetYourGuide, Viator) — the safety net: instant
   confirmation, free cancellation, reviews. Worth it in high season, for
   scarce activities, or when the trip cannot absorb a bad surprise.

## What travelers report on forums

Forum threads are where the practical details live. The ones worth relaying:

- The hour that avoids the crowds and the cruise groups.
- Whether booking ahead is genuinely necessary or a waste of flexibility.
- Which operator runs the boat everyone else resells.
- Hidden costs: parking, port tax, mandatory guide, equipment rental.
- Whether the "free shuttle" exists or the walk is 20 minutes uphill.
- The seasonal closure or the strike day that ruins the plan.

Report these as one concrete sentence in `forum_tip`. Never invent a review, a
rating, a company name or a quote: write the practical consensus, not a source.

## Honesty rules

- Do not name a specific local agency unless it is a well-known institution
  (a national museum, a public bus company, an official site).
- Do not promise a price you cannot know. Give a range or a percentage.
- If booking ahead really is required (a timed-entry site, a single daily
  boat), say it plainly rather than pushing the cheaper route.
